UsersModerators · reviewers · approvers, from engineers to managers

Discovery meant learning the conventions before drawing: I reviewed the tools engineers already use, then ran interviews per role on where they fail. Experts and I mapped the logic in the user's language, not the code's, the same interviews-before-documentation approach as Use Case 1. User journeys and wireframes built up to tested Figma prototypes. The resulting views resolve three forces at once: industry conventions, user needs per role and business constraints.

Constraints · industry standards define what a review must record · integrate into the existing platform and data model · 1000s of requirements, 100s of people

05 · Key design decisions

The recurring question: how do formal-review conventions map onto data that already lives in the project? I designed the roles, states and views so a review is traceable without leaving the requirements.

Assign groups, not individualsreviews repeat across a project
Rating per object, loggedapprover closes with a timestamp
Views per rolefilters for specialists and deciders

06 · Lessons learned

Involve experts and expert users early, design earlier still.

01
Design even earlierWhat I'd add: a middle step of first code prototypes, feeding development constraints back into the designs before full implementation.
02
Views and filters per roleCrucial were the different views and filters per role, the same thread I carried through Use Case 2. Improving the discovery would mean testing with different companies and different roles, hard in B2B, but crucial to avoid optimising within a narrow space. Not necessary for this MVP, but an important next step before adjusting to individual users' wishes.
