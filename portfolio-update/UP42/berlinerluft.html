Berliner Luft

How can a user understand Berlin's air pollution data in more depth without too much complexity?

Context
I started this project to combine data and user experience.
Scope
Research · data analysis in Python · sketches · HTML draft · coded prototype · Figma design system draft
Data
Hourly values · 15 stations · 2025 · NO₂, PM2.5
Outcome
Started 5 October 2026 · interface prototype (opens in a new tab) published 20 October 2026

By today’s EU limit, every station is fine. By the 2030 limit and WHO, many are not. Other interfaces show only today’s verdict, although the data are public.

Annual means 2025. Short peaks can still harm health, and a station 100 m or several km away cannot show personal exposure.

The interface

The user can select stations, months and weekdays or weekends to compare values over the day and year against current and future limits and guide values. Selection works by label or directly in the graph. Graph and map update each other.

How it works

Switch from weekday to weekend and the NO₂ rush-hour peaks drop, especially in the morning. Select stations, and central traffic sites stand out from suburb and background stations.

NO₂ peaks in the morning and evening and is much lower on weekend mornings. PM2.5 is highest in February and March. NO₂ varies by street and hour, PM2.5 by month, so the chart switches between hour and month.

Key design decisions

  • Station cardOne click opens a station card: type, distance, height, verdict per limit.
  • Three labelled limitsThree limits instead of a grade: EU now, EU 2030, WHO. “Good” becomes relative.
  • Missing data labelledGaps are named, not hidden: “not measured” in the table, “not published” on the card.
  • Extremes with contextExtremes show date, time, station and known events like New Year fireworks.

Lessons learned

  1. Data skills for design

    Understanding data, data analysis and data visualization helps me design information and interfaces more broadly, and integrate data work into design.

  2. AI in the design process

    Useful for research, finding data and coding a realistic prototype from a sketch. It struggles with consistency across states: layouts jump, labels overlap, backgrounds change. It clutters with grey explanations and works best with clear instructions or examples.

  3. Transparency and usability

    With more AI and more data everywhere, people want to verify. Balancing transparency and usability matters more than giving more answers.

  4. What I would test next

    Compare Berliner Luft, Apple Weather and Berlin LQI with one defined user group. Ask the same questions on speed and clarity: when is air better or worse, and which limits are changing? The other two answer “good now” fast.

  5. Next version · not built

    One place, several sources: stations, Berlin’s model grid, Sentinel-5P NO₂ and a satellite image of green vs. built-up areas, shown as context, not as a cause. Later, ozone, weather and health context from existing models, making information accessible and usable rather than competing with data modelling.
