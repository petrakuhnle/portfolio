02 · The shipped feature

In engineering for hardware products like aeroplanes, an engineer works with requirements, especially as a requirements or systems engineer. To manage the impact of one changing requirement on another across a project, we built the Master–Follower feature.

A Master requirement impacts dependent Follower requirements. Instead of tracking those changes in separate software, meetings and emails, we integrated it into the system.

03 · How it works

A change on the Master flags every Follower for its owner to apply or reject, with the decision logged.

A Master change reaches each Follower as its own state to review

The changes in the Master requirement propagate automatically. A key decision was to allow different states, synced, pending, rejected, so a change left pending too long or rejected can still be acted on. The feature also lets everyone involved communicate in place about the changes and decisions.

05 · Key design decisions

My job was to ask the specific questions: what does the engineer working on a Follower need to see, what would frustrate them, what does the Master's editor need versus the Follower's owner? Same user focus, but for different people in different situations. I build from the user and visual side so developers can focus on the technical side, and we meet in the middle. This role-and-view thinking is taken furthest in Use Case 3, Review Center.

06 · Lessons learned

What the first big feature taught me.

01
Design filled the gapI built the logic from interviews and user stories, using design to align earlier on detail developers couldn't guess, design filled the gap between high-level feature decisions and development.
02
A missing middle stepIdeally there would have been a middle step, more user testing and detailed feasibility alignment, rather than going prototype → contract → build. Part startup reality, part outside my influence at the time.
03
The feedback loopThis first, extensive feature was also my education in requirements and systems engineering. Because I had to design what I was told and show it back to the same experts a week later, I paid closer attention than listening alone allows, and later caught a detail that prevented a development refactor.
04
Carried into the next featureOn the next feature I asked for user-story collaboration and agreement on the most crucial requirements from the start. See Use Case 2, Verification Assignment Matrix.
