Discovery meant learning the conventions before drawing: I reviewed the tools engineers already use, then ran interviews per role on where they fail. Experts and I mapped the logic in the user's language, the same interviews-before-documentation approach as Use Case 1. User journeys and wireframes built up to tested Figma prototypes. The resulting views balance three things: industry conventions, user needs per role and business constraints.

Key design decisions

The recurring question: how do formal-review conventions map onto data that already lives in the project? I designed the roles, states and views so a review is traceable without leaving the requirements.

  • Assign groups, not individualsreviews repeat across a project
  • Rating per object, loggedapprover closes with a timestamp
  • Views per rolefilters for specialists and deciders

Lessons learned

Bring expert users in early, and get to design sooner.

  1. Design even earlier

    What I'd add: a middle step of first code prototypes, feeding development constraints back into the designs before full implementation.

  2. Views and filters per role

    Crucial were the different views and filters per role, the same thread I carried through Use Case 2. Improving the discovery would mean testing with different companies and different roles, hard in B2B, but crucial to avoid optimising within a narrow space. Not necessary for this MVP, but an important next step before adjusting to individual users' wishes.
