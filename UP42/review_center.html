How it works

A formal review that stays traceable, run on the data already in the project.

Reviewers see the items assigned to them. Approvers see status per requirement set. Before, reviewers were picked one by one and status was chased by email outside the project.

Process

Interviews per role, notes and user stories, a user journey, wireframes to hi-fi, and testing with experts and users.

Company
Valispace GmbH · acquired by Altium 2023
Team
CPO · subject-matter experts · CTO · designer owning the design process
Users
Moderators, reviewers and approvers, from engineers to managers
Constraints
Standards define what a review must record · existing platform and data model · thousands of requirements, hundreds of people

I studied the review tools engineers already used, then interviewed each role about where they fail. Experts and I wrote the logic in the users' words. Wireframes grew into tested Figma prototypes.

Key design decisions

The question behind the design: how do formal review conventions fit the data already in the project?

  • Assign groups. Reviews repeat across a project
  • Rating per item, logged. The approver closes with a timestamp
  • Views per role. Filters for specialists and deciders

Lessons learned

Bring expert users in early, and get to design sooner.

  1. Design even earlier

    Next time I would add early code prototypes, so development constraints reach the design before the build.

  2. Test across companies

    Views per role made the difference. The next step would be testing with several companies and roles, hard in B2B, but it keeps a design from fitting only one customer.
