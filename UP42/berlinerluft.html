Berliner Luft

How can a user understand Berlin's air pollution data in more depth without too much complexity?

NO₂ annual mean 2025, 15 stations

The user can grasp that the EU 2030 will change significantly what is considered good enough air quality and that individual locations in Berlin were above that limit in 2025.

The interface

The user can select stations, months and weekdays or weekends to compare hourly values against current and future limits and guide values. Selection works by label or directly in the graph. Graph and map update each other.

  • NO₂gas, mainly traffic

How it works

Switch from weekday to weekend and the NO₂ rush-hour peaks change. Select stations and central traffic sites stand out from suburb and background stations.

Lessons learned

  1. Data skills for design

    Understanding data, data analysis and data visualization helps me design information and interfaces more broadly, and integrate data work into design.

  2. AI in the design process

    Useful for research, finding data and coding a realistic prototype from a sketch. It struggles with consistency across states: layouts jump, labels overlap, backgrounds change. It clutters with grey explanations and works best with clear instructions or examples.

  3. What I would test next

    Compare Berliner Luft, Apple Weather and Berlin LQI with one defined user group. Ask the same questions on speed and clarity: when is air better or worse, and which limits are changing? The other two answer “good now” fast.

  4. Next version · not built

    One place, several sources: stations, Berlin’s model grid, Sentinel-5P NO₂ and satellite maps of green vs. built-up areas, each with its resolution. Causes, ozone and weather stay out.
