Independent project

Berliner Luft

When and where are people in Berlin most exposed to air pollution?

Context
Independent project, 2026. Open city data, each value tied to a location and an hour.
Scope
Data exploration, sketch, interface design, working prototype.
Data
Hourly values from 15 Berlin measuring stations, 2025. NO₂ and PM2.5, three limits.
Published
20 October 2026. Prototype on Hugging Face.

Impact

  • One screentime, place and three limits in one view
  • Prototype online20 October 2026, full 2025 data

The result of a limit check depends on the limit used. For NO₂, 0 of 15 stations are above the EU limit in force, 5 of 15 above the EU 2030 limit and 10 of 15 above the WHO guideline. Public sources usually show one value and do not state the limit.

The interface draws all three limits on the station chart and labels each one.

The interface

Two inputs: time and place. Time controls sit on the left, the station map on the right. Below are the daily curve and the annual mean per station against the limits. A station selected on the map, in the list or in the bar chart is selected in all views.

  • NO₂gas, mainly traffic exhaust
  • PM2.5fine particles
  • ■ ● ◆traffic, background, suburb
  • WHO, EU 2030, EU in force

Encoding is identical in all views: color per pollutant, shape per station type, line style per limit

How it works

Time and place are the two inputs. Both charts update from them.

Time: full year or single months; all days, weekdays or weekends. Place: any set of stations, selected on the map or by station type. Selection works in both directions. A station clicked in the limit chart is also selected on the map.

Data exploration

I charted the data before designing the screen. NO₂ peaks in the morning rush hour. PM2.5 varies little over the day. A daily curve shows this directly, so the interface has no hour selector.

Sketch

First build

First implementation of the sketch. Issues found in use:

  • StationsSidebar list with truncated names. Now labeled on the map, filtered by type.
  • MapSymbols without names. Now map, list and limit chart share one selection.
  • TimeHour range 00:00 to 23:00. Now full year or month; all days, weekdays or weekends.
  • LimitsOne limit at a time in a dropdown. Now all three drawn on the station chart.

Constraint: 15 measuring points for 892 km². The interface shows values per station and gives the distance to the nearest other station on selection. It shows no values per district.

Key design decisions

Each value has a location and a timestamp. The layout keeps both visible at all times.

  • Single dense screentime, place, daily curve and station comparison without navigation
  • Context next to dataeach pollutant card states source and health effect; details further down
  • Shared selectionmap, list and limit chart select the same stations
  • Labeled limitsdotted WHO, dashed EU 2030, solid EU in force
  • No hour selectorthe daily curve covers time of day
  • Missing data labeledstations without a PM2.5 sensor show “not measured here”
  • Station distancedistance to the nearest station shown on selection

Lessons learned

Data exploration defined the controls.

  1. Exploration before layout

    The charts showed which filters are useful: weekday or weekend, month. Single hours are not.

  2. Charts as first draft

    Colors, line styles and sort order from the exploration charts were reused in the interface.

  3. AI with narrow scope

    AI was useful for quick context research, for example current and future limits per pollutant. Results improved once design decisions were fixed and questions were narrow.

  4. Next

    Compare today's live value with 2025 values for the same hour. Usability test with residents.
