How I turned 236,180 hourly measurements into an interface for the air Berliners breathe

A self-initiated data project. I framed the question, pulled and cleaned a full year from Berlin’s 15 air quality stations in Python, and designed the interface from paper sketch to coded version 1.

Berliner Luft , NO₂ and PM2.5 · 15 stations · every hour of 2025 · v1 October 2026

Choose a time and a place; the two charts answer when and where the air is worst.

01 · Finding

Within EU limitsall 15 stations stayed below today’s annual limits of 40 µg/m³ NO₂ and 25 µg/m³ PM2.5
Above WHO, 12 of 12every station that measures fine particles sat above the WHO guideline of 5 µg/m³; for NO₂, 10 of 15 were above 10
5 traffic stationswould fail the EU 2030 NO₂ limit of 20, led by Mariendorfer Damm at 26.3 µg/m³

Whether Berlin’s air is “OK” depends on the standard you read it against. The interface shows all three side by side instead of deciding for the reader. Annual means from provisional, unvalidated hourly data.

02 · The interface

Berlin measures air quality at 15 points across 892 km². A phone app shows one number for the city or for where you stand, but between the stations that number is an estimate, and a daily figure hides how much the air changes between 4 am and the evening rush.

The interface keeps to what was measured: when in the day the air is worst, where in the city, and how far each station is from the limits.

05 · Key design decisions

The question behind every choice: what can this data honestly answer? The stations are accurate; the space between them is not measured. The interface shows the first and never paints over the second.

Two pollutantsNO₂ for the street, PM2.5 for the city
All 15 stationsthe spread is the point
Hour of day firsta daily average flattens the rush hour
Three limits, not one verdictWHO, EU 2030, EU now
Nothing by colour aloneshape for type, line style for limits
Gaps stay gapsdays under 18 valid hours left out

06 · Lessons learned

What working with real data taught me.

01
The data designs the menuWhich views make sense depends on what the file holds: hourly timestamps unlock hour of day, one station disables comparison. Designing from the data’s properties came before drawing screens.
02
Coding the iterations exposed the layoutOverlapping labels and broken names only showed up with all 15 real stations on screen. A static mockup with three tidy dots would have hidden both.
03
Accuracy and coverage are different claimsSaying “the measurements are inaccurate” would have been wrong. Fifteen accurate points, nothing between them is the honest version.
04
AI + humanAI found the station API, ruled out satellite NO₂ (a column, not street-level air) and drafted code. The question, the scope, the sketches and every design iteration were mine.
