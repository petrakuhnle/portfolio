About my writing

From 2016 to 2020 I worked at AppleCare, first in iTunes & App Store support and then as a Tier 4 Pro Apps Advisor for Logic Pro and Final Cut Pro. Every day I took users through Apple's troubleshooting guides in German and English, by chat, email and phone, and put each step in words that fit the person. When a guide was unclear or out of date, I reported it. I received the AppleCare Excellence Award in 2018 for my chat work.

Since then I've written user stories and specs as a product designer, and contributed audio documentation to MDN and librosa. I'm German native, have lived and worked mainly in English since 2012, and speak Dutch fluently. Training: Google Technical Writing and an Alison technical writing course.

MDN Web Docs: AudioSession introduction

Merged 4 May 2026 · mdn/content #43992

I shortened the introduction from three sentences to one and added what the user actually hears: other audio pauses, gets quieter, or keeps playing.

Before

The AudioSession interface of the Audio Session API allows developers to specify how audio from a web application interacts with other audio playing on a device.

An audio session represents the aggregated audio output from a web page. It allows web pages to express the general nature of their audio output, such as playback, recording, or transient sounds like notifications. The platform can then use this information to determine how web-based audio should interact with other applications on the device, for example, whether web audio should pause other audio or play alongside it.

After

The AudioSession interface of the Audio Session API lets a web page declare the type of audio it is producing — for example music playback, a video call, or a short notification. The platform uses that declaration to decide how the page's audio and audio from other applications and tabs should coexist — whether to pause, duck (lower the volume), or play in parallel.

  • The old text said “how audio interacts” twice.
  • “Aggregated audio output” and “general nature” are now concrete examples.
  • I used the audio term duck and explained it in brackets.
  • Other browser tabs were missing, so I added them.

Help topic for beginners and pros

Recording latency is a typical Pro Apps support case. I wrote it once for someone new to recording and once for a professional.

For a new user: If you hear yourself late when you record

When you play or sing, you might hear your own sound a moment after you make it. This delay is called latency. You can make it shorter:

  1. Choose Logic Pro > Settings > Audio, then click Devices.
  2. Choose a smaller I/O Buffer Size, such as 128.
  3. Play a few notes. If you hear clicks or crackles, or see a System Overload message, choose the next larger size.

When you finish recording and start mixing, set the buffer size back to a larger value. Mixing uses more of your Mac's processing power, and a larger buffer gives it room.

For a professional: Reduce monitoring latency

Round-trip latency depends on the I/O buffer size, the sample rate, and your audio interface's driver.

  • Set I/O Buffer Size in Settings > Audio > Devices. Use 32–128 samples to track and 512–1024 to mix.
  • At the same buffer size, a higher sample rate gives lower latency, but uses more CPU.
  • Plug-ins with lookahead, such as linear-phase EQs and some limiters, add delay to every track. Bypass them while tracking.
  • If your interface has direct (zero-latency) monitoring, use it, and mute the input in Logic so you don't hear yourself twice.
  • The beginner version explains one term and says what to watch out for.
  • The pro version skips the basics and lists the trade-offs.
  • In Apple support I had to pick one of these approaches at the start of every conversation.

How I write with LLMs

I use LLMs a lot, including for my portfolio and this page. I always check the result against what I meant.

  1. I write the first version myself.
  2. An LLM edits it. I tell it who the reader is and which style rules apply.
  3. I compare the result with my original, line by line, often with Claude.

Things I often have to fix

  • Specific words turn vague: “duck (lower the volume)” becomes “adjust the audio”.
  • A condition or exception disappears, often the one the user needed.
  • A fact changes meaning, for example “samples” becomes “sound levels”.
  • Menu names and settings are made up, so I check them in the app.
LLM “improvement”

Adjust your buffer settings as needed for optimal performance.

My version, kept

When you start mixing, set the buffer size back to a larger value.

The LLM version reads well but no longer says when, or bigger or smaller. I made this example up for the page.
